Budget flow map

How the question flow adapts. Each starting point walks the same topic modules in a different order, every answer can open or close the next questions, and checks fire once the whole picture is known. Built from the working graph in the repo and validated by walking it.

Validation result

Passed. 109 questions, 50,000 randomised walks across 4 starting points and 5 data profiles, plus 11 scripted scenarios. Every question is reachable, every option leads somewhere, no walk repeats a question, asks before the answer it depends on, or returns to a topic it left. Every walk then builds a real plan, and each plan obeys the real-life rules below.

Problems the walks found and I fixed
  • Questions drifting back to an earlier topic once the short-run budget changed: added a cursor so a topic is never reopened.
  • A follow-up asked without its parent (regular medication without health cover): follow-ups now require the parent answer.
  • Debt-intent users with tracked debts were asked "what kinds of debt?" as if they had none: now "What else do you owe?" after confirming.
  • Mortgage holders were asked "Do you owe money anywhere?": now "Besides your mortgage, do you owe money anywhere?", and the same for a financed car.
  • A car loan could be counted twice (once as a loan, once as a car payment): a single "Is the car payment one of the loans you listed?" question settles it.
  • Confirm cards for income, bills, debts and goals were scattered across topics: they are now one "What I found" screen at the start.
  • Follow-up of a check (extra income after a deficit) landed after the priorities screen: it now follows the check directly.
  • Long runs: late in a run only questions that move the plan a lot are asked, and related fields share a screen.

Goals and the checks that need the whole picture

These fire after the topics they depend on are finished, so the question is based on real numbers, not guesses.

  G1["What is it? Cost, saved so far, by when or how much monthly"] --> CK{"Fits what the plan can spare?"}
  CK -->|No| AD["Extend, lower, save more or keep"]
  AD -->|Extend| E1["New number of months"]
  AD -->|Lower| E2["New target"]
  CK -->|Yes, with room| AN["Add another goal?"]
  CK --> SF{"Little saved, or costly debt?"}
  SF -->|Yes| GS["Goal first, balanced or safety first"]
  X["Fixed costs leave almost nothing?"] -->|Yes| DF["What could change? Lower rent, refinance, extra income, pause goals, family help"]
  Y["Slack under 12 percent?"] -->|Yes| CT["What to trim first?"]
  Z["Slack over 12 percent?"] -->|Yes| SL["Save hard or live comfortably?"]

Rules every plan must obey

Checked on every one of the walks, not just the scripted people.

  • Every line is zero or more, the lines add up to income, and nothing is left unassigned. A shortfall always carries a warning.
  • An owner never gets a rent line, and a paid-off home never gets a mortgage line.
  • Overdue payments are cleared first, over three months.
  • Self-employed income reserves tax before spending (15% unless the person says otherwise).
  • Savings never exceed 90% of income, and a big surplus never sits in a buffer while the safety net is still empty.

What the engine now applies

RuleWhat the plan does
Safety net3 months of essentials for a steady single income, 6 with dependants, varying pay or an uncertain job, 9 for self-employed with dependants. Under one month saved, it is filled in about two months.
Costly debt firstAny debt at 15% or more, or a card with an unknown rate (assumed 25%), takes most of the spare money before extra saving or investing.
Payoff orderHighest interest first when rates are known, smallest balance first if the person chooses it. Instalments never get extra.
Debt shareWarns above 36% of income. Above 50% it says a budget alone cannot fix it.
Needs shareWarns when essentials pass 60% of income (the 50/30/20 guide is 50%).
GoalsNever take more than 35% of income. Bigger goals are stretched and the plan says so. Safety first halves the goal pace until one month is saved.
Variable incomePlans on the slowest month. Seasonal and freelance income reserve tax first.
Yearly and coming costsYearly bills and a baby, wedding, move or car are spread across the months as a set-aside.
RetirementIf there is none and the basics are in place, a small start of up to 5% of income.
Saving targetA target the person sets is filled from everyday spending, never below the essential floors.

How it learns

  • Edits: when someone changes an everyday line or the safety net on a plan Buddgy built, the engine moves that line toward what they chose. One edit counts a little; three trusted signals count fully. Nothing moves further than 40% from the standard estimate.
  • Month close: where real spending missed a cap by a clear margin, that teaches more than an edit. Spending within 10% of the cap teaches nothing.
  • Rebuilds: a plan rebuilt within two weeks of the last build is counted. Repeated rebuilds make Buddgy confirm assumptions first.
  • Memory: the stable answers (income type, housing, partner share, cover, debt order) and the learned habits live on the synced profile, so every device and Buddgy chat read the same facts. Buddgy gets them as short sentences, rebuilt each time, so they are never stale or duplicated.
