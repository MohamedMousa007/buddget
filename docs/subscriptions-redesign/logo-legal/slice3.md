## Reasons
- **Spotify** (YELLOW): Design guidelines govern partners using Spotify content/metadata (attribution with logo). No clause for a tracker referencing the service. Forbids altering the logo and pairing Spotify with other brands. Not a Spotify integration, so guidelines apply only by analogy. Wordmark-as-text safer.
- **Apple Music** (RED): Apple says third parties need an express written trademark licence for Apple-owned logos/graphic symbols; only word marks may be used referentially (e.g. 'compatible with') and less prominently than own name. Page does not name the Apple Music logo specifically; general rule applied. Plain text name is acceptable.
- **Apple Arcade** (RED): Same Apple rule: logos need express written licence; text word mark referential use only. Same note as Apple Music.
- **PlayStation Plus** (YELLOW): Sony notice lists marks and reserves rights; gives no third-party usage rules; points to an Official Licensing Program page (not read). Sony is a known IP enforcer; marks are stylised. Conservative fallback.
- **Xbox Game Pass** (RED): Microsoft guidelines say not to use its logos, icons or designs in any manner without permission; word marks may be used to accurately describe a product without implying endorsement. xbox.com/en-US/legal/trademarks returned 404. Text name OK.
- **Nintendo Switch Online** (RED): Nintendo's copyright page (read directly) bars third-party use of its logos/trademarks with non-Nintendo products or services or where customers could be confused, and says it generally declines permission requests. Known aggressive enforcer. Plain-text name only.
- **Discord Nitro** (YELLOW): Discord marks need permission for most uses; allowed to state you use Discord without implying affiliation; do not edit/recolour. No tracker-specific clause. Weak fit with the 'say you are a Discord user' allowance. Wordmark text safer.
- **Twitch Turbo / subs** (YELLOW): Brand page only offers an official asset download; no stated rules on third-party use or permission found. Full guidelines not located.
- **LinkedIn Premium** (YELLOW): LinkedIn allows use only as described in its guidelines or with express written permission, official assets only, must not suggest sponsorship; may ask you to stop. Close to RED on strictness.
- **Duolingo Super** (YELLOW): Duolingo design site gives logo colour/clear-space/no-recolour rules but nothing on third-party permission; ToS reportedly grants no trademark licence (secondary summary). Mascot is distinctive; character art riskier than wordmark.
- **Udemy (courses / Personal Plan)** (RED): Udemy trademark guidelines (search summary of official support page; direct fetch returned 403) require Udemy written permission for its logos. Primary page still unreadable (HTTP 403); not confirmed first-hand.
- **Snapchat+** (YELLOW): Snap Kit guidelines name the Ghost logo as something third-party apps must not use as their own branding; ads may not use Snap trademarks outside brand guidelines. No general referential-use rule found. Context is developer/ad programmes, not identification use; leaning RED if challenged.
- **Anghami** (YELLOW): No public brand guidelines found. SDK licence says Anghami is a trademark and marks must follow guidelines supplied under a developer agreement. Unofficial logo aggregators are not a licence.
- **Deezer** (YELLOW): Official guidelines (reportedly deezerbrand.com) not retrieved; only aggregator pages found, one marking the logo editorial-use-only. Check deezerbrand.com manually.
- **Audible** (YELLOW): No Audible-specific policy found. Amazon's ad policy says Amazon marks beyond the 'available at Amazon' logo need written approval; its trademark guidelines target licensees and bar altering logos or implying endorsement. Amazon is a heavy brand enforcer; policy is for advertising/licensees, not identification use.
- **Storytel** (YELLOW): No official logo usage guide found; press room exists at press.storytel.com (not inspected for terms). 
- **Kindle Unlimited** (YELLOW): No Kindle-specific logo policy found. Amazon's ad policy lists 'Kindle' among terms third parties may not use in ads without approval. Same Amazon caveats as Audible.
- **YouTube Music Premium** (YELLOW): YouTube brand hub (via search summary) requires approval via a form for some contexts, forbids altering the logo, and says the logo should not be used alone without approval; API branding rules forbid implying endorsement. YouTube Music icon not specifically covered. Google 'Third Party Use' terms require consent for uncovered uses.
- **Scribd / Everand** (YELLOW): No official Scribd/Everand brand guidelines found. Owner's brand pages not located.
- **EA Play** (YELLOW): No EA Play logo policy found; EA's tournament guidelines forbid implying EA endorsement and note EA has not cleared third-party content. 
- **Ubisoft+** (YELLOW): No Ubisoft logo/Ubisoft+ policy found; video policy covers game footage only. 
- **Google Play Pass** (RED): Google pages (via search summary) say uses beyond the guidelines need prior written consent, Google Play-mark creatives need brand-team approval, and Google logos must not be used in app icons. Guidelines target developers promoting their apps; Google Brand Resource Center not read. Could soften to YELLOW on review. Text name acceptable.
- **Roblox Premium** (RED): Roblox usage guidelines (via search summary) say the logo is not allowed except via the official badge, limited to select partners, and unsolicited requests are not granted. 
- **Fortnite Crew** (YELLOW): Epic bars creators from using Fortnite/Epic logos in experiences or their promotion; says third-party logos generally need the rightsholder's permission. No rule for trackers. Creator-programme terms apply only by analogy; leaning RED.
- **Coursera Plus** (YELLOW): Coursera press page (read) has no usage rules; points to a press kit and press@coursera.org. No explicit permission or prohibition found.
- **Skillshare** (YELLOW): ToS reportedly grants no right to use Skillshare trademarks/logos (secondary summary only). Primary ToS not read.
- **MasterClass** (YELLOW): No official guidelines found; affiliate material says supplied creatives must be used as-is (secondary). 
- **Telegram Premium** (YELLOW): No official Telegram brand guidelines found; ToS page read contains none. Check telegram.org/tos/brand manually.
- **X Premium** (YELLOW): Only developer display requirements found (X logo shown with X content); no general third-party logo policy; brand toolkit page returned 402. 
- **Tinder** (YELLOW): No official brand/press guidelines found. Match Group is an active IP enforcer; flame is a distinctive mark.
- **Bumble** (YELLOW): No official brand/press guidelines found.
