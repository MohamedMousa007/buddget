## RED
- Amazon Prime (Egypt): Amazon Trademark Usage Guidelines are written for licensees/partners with express permission; marks only in materials approved in advance in writing; no altering. Prime Video partner guide allows only the text 'Available on Prime Video', not the logo. Contact trademarks@amazon.com. Notes: Guidelines target licensees, not referential use, so arguably not directly applicable, but the written-approval requirement is explicit. Text name is safer.
- Apple TV (Apple TV+): Apple's guidelines say the Apple logo or other Apple-owned symbols may not be used by third parties without an express written licence; only word marks may be used in a referential phrase, not as part of a product name, and without implying endorsement. Notes: Clear published prohibition on the logo. Wordmark-as-text is the safer alternative.
- YouTube Premium: YouTube brand site (per search summaries of current and archived versions) requires special approval via a Brand Use Request Form for use of YouTube brand elements and bars standalone logo use; Google's guidelines require permission for product icons and bar implying endorsement. Notes: brand.youtube page text itself could not be read by fetch; approval requirement taken from search snippets and archived versions - verify manually. Google brand is actively policed.
- The New York Times: NYT trademark policy: marks may be referenced only for its own products; logos and design marks require prior written authorization; no implying sponsorship. Contact rights@nytimes.com. Precedent: 2009 NYT counsel demanded news aggregator Newser stop using its logo. Notes: Explicit prohibition plus enforcement history against an aggregator.
- The Economist: A third-party copy of Economist Terms of Use says its trademarks incl. name and logos may not be used for any purpose without written permission from The Economist Group. Notes: economist.com was blocked to fetch; clause taken from a mirror, verify on the official site. Conservative RED pending verification.
