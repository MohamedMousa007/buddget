# Buddget: third-party logos in a subscription tracker - legal and platform framework

Research assistant output, NOT legal advice. Date: 2026-10-17. Research was cut short by a session limit; items marked [UNVERIFIED] were not confirmed against primary text and must be checked by counsel. All URLs read 2026-10-17 (via search summaries or fetch).

## 1. Trademark law

### US
- Nominative fair use (New Kids on the Block v. News America, 9th Cir. 1992): use of a mark to refer to the trademark owner's own product is OK if (1) the thing is not readily identifiable without the mark, (2) only as much of the mark is used as reasonably necessary, (3) nothing suggests sponsorship/endorsement. Playboy v. Welles (9th Cir. 2002) applied it; stylised wallpaper use of the marks fell outside it. Toyota v. Tabari (9th Cir. 2010): broker's use of "Lexus" in domains was nominative; Sleekcraft confusion test displaced; logo had been removed and a disclaimer added before appeal (disclaimer/dropping the logo helped).
  Sources: https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/279/279.F3d.796.00-55538.00-55537.00-55229.00-55009.html ; https://web.mst.edu/canisr/legalenvironment/cases/welles.pdf ; https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/publications/2016/11/2016_11_16_second_circuit_expands_split__13324 ; https://www.lexplug.com/casebrief/toyota_motor_sales_u_s_a_inc_v_tabari_659bc3752423c3ba321f01ca
- Caveat: the 2nd/3rd circuits treat nominative use as a factor in confusion analysis rather than a separate defence (Arnold & Porter link). Key point for logos: the tests were developed mostly for WORD marks; using the full logo (not just the name) is weaker under prong 2 ("only so much as necessary") - the name in plain text usually suffices. A logo tile is a closer call than text; recognition-at-a-glance is the argument for necessity.
- Closest analogue: bank suits against Plaid (TD Bank 2020 - settled and dismissed; PNC 2020 - summary judgment denied for both sides, fact questions on confusion, trial outcome NOT verified). Plaid's defence was that logos are references so users pick the right bank. Relevant lesson: claims were about logos + colour schemes used in a flow that looked like the bank's own login, i.e. implied affiliation. A passive tracker tile is lower-risk but the suits show big brands do sue.
  Sources: https://www.bankingdive.com/news/td-plaid-lawsuit-trademark-infringement-false-advertising/587103/ ; https://www.americanbanker.com/payments/news/pnc-sues-plaid-for-trademark-infringement ; https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/pnc-plaid-denied-summary-judgment-in-trademark-suit

### EU / UK
- EUTMR Art. 14(1)(c) / Directive 2015/2436 Art. 14: proprietor cannot stop use of the mark to identify or refer to the proprietor's goods/services, or to indicate intended purpose, if in accordance with honest commercial practices. 2015 reform dropped the express "necessary" wording; national courts differ on whether necessity survives (https://www.maastrichtuniversity.nl/blog/2021/01/name-dropping-marketing).
- BMW v Deenik (C-63/97) and Gillette (C-228/03): referential use is fine unless it creates impression of commercial connection, takes unfair advantage of reputation, denigrates, or presents goods as imitation (https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/use-of-third-party-trade-marks-permissible-provided-use-is-honest ; https://www.ippt.eu/sites/ippt/files/1999/IPPT19990223_ECJ_BMW_v_Deenik.pdf). Note summaries disagree on BMW judgment date (1998/1999); verify.
- UK Trade Marks Act 1994 s.11(2)(c) (intended purpose, honest practices) and s.10(6) (referring to proprietor's goods): https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1994/26/section/11 . Nokia v Wärtsilä not verified.
- Buddget has no stated EU/UK users, so this is persuasive background only; but Apple/Google review is global.

### Egypt (Law 82/2002)
- Trademark = sign distinguishing goods/services, must be visually perceptible (Art. 63); foreign WTO nationals can register (Art. 62). Law replaced Law 57/1939. Source (summary only): https://www.agip.com/Agip-Country-Service/en/30/T/2 ; https://alp.unescwa.org/legislations/law-82-2002-intellectual-property (403 on fetch).
- Not verified: Article 66 text (exclusive right) and any express referential/nominative exception; unfair-competition provisions (Art. 66 is cited in practice but I could not confirm). [UNVERIFIED] Assume NO US-style nominative fair use doctrine is codified; defences would rest on absence of confusion/ use "in trade as a mark". Egyptian practitioners should confirm. Logo artwork is also copyright-protected under the same law (Book 3).

### Saudi Arabia
- GCC Unified Trademark Law adopted by Royal Decree M/51 (1435H), in force 2016, with SAIP executive regulations (https://atblegal.com/blog/intellectual-property-laww/trademark-law-uae-saudi-qatar-bahrain/ ; https://saudipedia.com/en/law-of-trademarks ). I found NO evidence of a distinct 2023 Saudi trademark statute; SAIP issued regulations (geographic names, Dec 2023) and joined Nice 12 / Madrid steps (https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1694764 ; https://gpg-pdf.chambers.com/Doing-Business-In-2026/926/). Exception text for referential use not found [UNVERIFIED].

### UAE
- Federal Decree-Law 36/2021, in force 2 Jan 2022, replaced Law 37/1992; Cabinet Decision 57/2022 regs (https://tamimi.com/news/uae-issues-new-trademarks-law ; https://paoletti.com/the-new-trademark-law-of-the-uae/ ). Full text 403 on fetch (https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1535/download). Exceptions/limits article NOT confirmed [UNVERIFIED]. Unfair-competition / commercial-fraud law not researched.
- Gulf practice: authorities and courts tend to be protective of well-known marks; telecoms and banks are active enforcers [general knowledge, unverified].

### Factors raising risk (synthesis)
1. Logo as decoration/branding rather than identification (e.g. in marketing, hero art, onboarding).
2. Anything implying partnership, "official", "connected", "powered by" (esp. banks: "Bank-linked" UI).
3. Logos in store screenshots/listing (prominent commercial promotion; also Apple 5.2.x review).
4. Altered colours, cropping, glow/animation, placing inside your own brand frame.
5. Using app icons / Apple, Google logos (Apple restricts the Apple logo for developers; word marks allowed for compatibility - https://www.apple.com/legal/intellectual-property/guidelinesfor3rdparties.html).
6. Copyright in logo artwork (separate from trademark; nominative fair use does NOT cover copyright; complex logos/mascots riskier than wordmarks; copyright in the SVG file as redrawn by a third party e.g. icon packs).
7. Arabic marks/ stylised Arabic wordmarks: same analysis; name in plain Arabic text is lower risk than the logo.
8. Competitors of your own future features (e.g. offering a competing product to the brand: BNPL, wallet) could weaken "no confusion" narrative.
9. Notice-and-ignore: keeping a logo after a complaint turns innocent use into wilful.

## 2. Platform policy
- Apple 5.2.1: no third-party trademarks/copyrights without permission; 5.2.2: if app displays content from a third-party service, that service's terms must allow it, be ready to prove authorization; 5.2.4/5.2.5 Apple endorsement and look-alike; 4.1: no other developer's icon/brand in your app icon/name. Source: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ . Practice: reviewers rarely flag small logos in lists; flagging occurs on complaint or on listing/icon/screenshots.
- Google Play IP policy: avoid brand names/logos without permission, especially where it could confuse; apps may not infringe or induce infringement; owners usually contact developer first then file complaint. Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888072 . Impersonation policy not read.
- Google brand: informational plain-text references allowed; logo use governed by Brand Resource Center / API terms: https://about.google/brand-resource-center/guidance/apis/
- In-practice display by Rocket Money, Bobby, Monarch, YNAB, Emma, Copilot, Egyptian/GCC apps: NOT researched (session limit). General knowledge: US aggregators (Plaid/Yodlee/MX) show institution logos supplied by data vendors, often with vendor disclaimers; subscription trackers (Bobby, Rocket Money) show service logos widely, sourced from logo APIs or hand-curated. Treat as [UNVERIFIED, tolerated in practice, not proof of legality].

## 3. Logo sources
- Brandfetch terms: logos belong to owners ("Third-Party IP"), Brandfetch disclaims liability, grants only a limited licence to the service/content NOT to the underlying marks; customer must refresh at least every 30 days and delete expired cache; customer indemnifies Brandfetch for own infringement; removal requests handled at Brandfetch's discretion. => it does NOT license trademarks; risk stays with you. https://brandfetch.com/terms . Its "Guide to Logo Fair Use" could not be fetched.
- logo.dev: terms page not retrieved (404s). Clearbit free Logo API shut down Dec 2025 (HubSpot changelog: https://developers.hubspot.com/changelog/upcoming-sunset-of-clearbits-free-logo-api); logo.dev positions as replacement and previously required attribution for free tier (https://www.logo.dev/clearbit). Assume same risk allocation as Brandfetch; read terms directly. [UNVERIFIED]
- Simple Icons: repo is CC0 but individual icons may have other licences; disclaims responsibility for brand legal action; tells users to get permissions and follow brand guidelines; removals on request. https://github.com/simple-icons/simple-icons/blob/develop/DISCLAIMER.md
- Wikimedia Commons: copyright tags are separate from "Trademarked" notices; trademark may restrict use anyway. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Template:Wikimedia_trademark (illustrative)
- Brand guidelines (high level): Netflix assets: follow guidelines, do not alter/recolour/distort (https://help.netflix.com/legal/media-terms-and-conditions). Spotify: partner/attribution guidelines, full logo for integrations, colour rules (https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/design). Apple: logo restricted to licensees/resellers; word marks OK for compatibility. Microsoft/Amazon/Vodafone/noon/banks: not researched; typically "press kit for editorial use, not for product UI".
- Takeaway: no cheap source gives you a trademark licence. API = convenient hosting + takedown channel + file-quality, not legal cover.

## 4. Options matrix
| Option | Legal risk | Store risk | Cost/effort | UX |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a. Official logos only where guidelines permit (Spotify, Netflix, Google where API terms apply) | Lowest for those brands | Low (can show proof, 5.2.2) | Medium (per-brand review) | Good |
| b. Initials/category icon for everyone else | None | None | Low | Okay |
| c. Runtime licensed logo API | Medium (risk stays with you, but fresh, removable, takedown channel) | Low-Med | Low | Great |
| d. Mono Lucide-style category icons | None | None | Low | Okay, generic |
| e. User-uploaded icons | Shifts to user; you are host (DMCA/notice-and-takedown style duty) | Low-Med (UGC rules) | Medium | Flexible |
| f. Written permission / partnership | None | None | High, slow, only for big players | Best |
Recommendation: a+b+c(mitigated) as default, d as fallback, e as the user escape hatch, f opportunistically for 5-10 top brands (Vodafone, Orange, e&, STC, noon, banks).

Mitigations: in-app + Terms disclaimer; takedown email + 24h SLA; no logos in store screenshots/marketing/app icon/onboarding hero; per-brand remote-config kill switch (flag per brand id -> falls back to initials); logos from CDN/API or OTA bundle, not baked in binary (so removal needs no store release); render unmodified, in neutral tile, never recoloured; show brand NAME in text next to logo (supports necessity/identification); no logos on bank-login or "connect" flows; keep a dated log of each logo's source and any permission.

## 5. Policy proposal
Tier A - logo allowed: brand publishes guidelines/press kit allowing referential or developer use, or API terms we have accepted (e.g. Spotify, Netflix, Google where terms apply); use unmodified asset in permitted colourway, no implied partnership. Also: global SaaS/streaming where the service is the user's own recurring charge and the name is displayed beside it.
Tier B - logo allowed with conditions: no published rule; logo fetched at runtime from vendor API (not bundled), shown only inside the user's own subscription row/picker at <=40px, never in marketing, kill switch per brand, honour any complaint within 24h; applies to telecoms, retailers (noon), most services. Banks, payment brands and government-like entities start in Tier B with extra care (no login-like UI) or Tier C if risk high.
Tier C - initials/category icon: Apple/Apple logo (use word mark/initials instead; Apple restricts the logo), any brand that has objected, any brand whose logo is copyright-complex or a mascot, any brand with a prior takedown, anything we cannot source from a documented place, and any entity where the logo could look like endorsement (banks in Egypt/KSA/UAE pending counsel). Default for unknown brands.

Draft in-app text (for lawyer review):
"Brand names and logos shown in Buddget belong to their respective owners and are used only to help you recognise the service a charge relates to. Buddget is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by any of them. If you own a brand shown here and want its logo changed or removed, email [legal address]; we aim to act within 24 hours."
Terms clause: "Third-Party Marks. All third-party names, logos and brands are the property of their owners and appear solely for identification. Their appearance does not imply affiliation or endorsement. Rights holders may request removal at [address]; we will remove or replace the asset promptly. You may not use such marks through Buddget for any purpose other than identifying your own subscriptions."

Pre-release checklist:
1. Brand list diffed against Tier table; new brands default Tier C.
2. No logos in store screenshots, icon, preview video, promo text.
3. Kill-switch config reachable and tested (flip one brand off -> initials appear without update).
4. Logos unmodified, name in text beside logo, no "official/partner/connected" wording.
5. Disclaimer present in Settings/About and Terms; takedown inbox monitored.
6. Apple: 5.2.2 proof file for Tier A brands; Play: IP-policy review of listing text.
7. Source log (URL, date, licence) updated; API cache refresh <= 30 days if Brandfetch.
8. No Apple logo or Apple-lookalike UI.

## Ten key risks
1. Full logos exceed "only as much as necessary" in some tests; text name might suffice.
2. API vendors do not license the marks; risk and indemnity sit with Buddget.
3. Implied endorsement, esp. with banks/telecoms (Plaid suits).
4. Copyright in logo artwork not covered by nominative fair use.
5. Egypt/KSA/UAE have no verified codified nominative exception; local counsel needed.
6. Store listing/screenshots/icon using logos (highest practical takedown trigger).
7. Apple logo/Apple look-alikes (5.2.4/5.2.5, Apple guidelines).
8. Apple 5.2.2 proof-of-authorization demand for displayed third-party service content.
9. Ignoring a complaint = wilfulness and store suspension risk.
10. Stale/altered/recoloured logos and logo APIs changing terms or shutting down (Clearbit, Dec 2025).
