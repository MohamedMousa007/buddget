1 · Campaign idea and the three actsPending approval
What's actually left?
فاضل فعلًا كام؟

One story told in three acts. The same question is asked, answered, then shown. Nothing changes except how much of Buddget the audience sees.

The insight owner belief

Everyone checks the bank balance. The balance is the number before the instalments, the subscriptions, the rent and the goals. What people really want to know is what is actually left. To be validated in interviews, not stated as a statistic.

The one-line story

A question we all ask quietly (Act 1). The idea that answers it (Act 2). The product that shows it (Act 3).

The through-line

The question mark resolves. In Act 1 the dotted leaders end in "?". In Act 2 the pieces line up into one page. In Act 3 a real screen shows what a real month looks like.

Act 1The Question

Objective
Recognition and shareability. Make people feel seen, and make the Buddget name and look familiar as the voice that asks the question. No product, no feature, no download ask.
Intended feeling
"That's me, and it's normal." Relief and a little amusement. Never guilt.
Central message
The balance isn't what's left. It's the number before you subtract everything that is already spoken for.
Leads to Act 2 by
Every post ends on the unfinished question. Act 2 opens with "what if you could see all of it at once?"
Example content
The "Day 25 to day 3" carousel; the 14-second "pause" Reel and TikTok; habit polls in Stories ("do you check your balance before you buy?"). Format: type, dotted leaders, mono numbers, the wordmark as sign-off, Buddgy only as a small closing mood.
Execution
Motion graphics and typography only. No person on camera. Voice-over by a human artist in the market's dialect. About 3 videos and 2 carousels a week, plus Stories.

Act 2One Picture

Objective
Establish the idea and the brand's philosophy: understanding comes from seeing everything together, not from guessing harder. Introduce Buddgy as a character and build trust and familiarity.
Intended feeling
"Oh. It could be simpler." Curiosity, ease, a sense of being understood.
Central message
One month. One picture. Money is complicated enough; seeing it shouldn't be.
Leads to Act 3 by
Ending on "we're building it", with the beta list as the only call to action, so the next step is looking at the thing itself.
Example content
The "pieces assemble" Reel (below); money notes ("what an instalment really costs you each month"); "Meet Buddgy"; a Stories question box ("what confuses you most about your money?").
Execution
The same visual system, more Buddgy, the first behind-the-scenes posts. No product claims yet beyond the positioning line.

Motion and animation style

  • Calm and precise. Roughly 1.5 to 2.5 seconds per beat. No whooshes, shakes or flashes.
  • The leaders draw on, left to right, as if being written in a ledger. Numbers tick into place.
  • The marker wipes across the key word.
  • Buddgy changes mood, he doesn't dance. Use the existing mood animations (blink, tilt, the face-in). The app eases the eyes with a soft curve, cubic-bezier(.2,.9,.25,1); reuse it.
  • Sound: one soft tick per subtraction, a pause on the question. No stock "money" sound effects.
  • A motion designer sets exact timings. These are directions, not verified figures.

How Buddget and Buddgy appear

Buddget appears as the wordmark in the sign-off position of every post, and as the mark for the avatar and Story covers. Buddgy appears small in a bottom corner or, in a few moments, centred as the post's single subject. Wordmark minimum 18px tall; no outline, shadow or recolour; keep it left to right.

The five recurring devices

1 · Mist paper

A soft off-white ground and ink type, like a ledger page. It makes Buddget look calm, not like a glossy fintech screen.

2 · Ledger leaders

Dotted lines tie a label to an amount, as on a receipt. They echo the coin b and carry the "adding it up" idea across all three acts.

3 · The marker

One key word per post gets a soft teal highlight. It focuses the eye and keeps one idea per post.

4 · One big question

Posts lead with a plain question or sentence, never a statistic or a promise.

5 · Mono numbers

Amounts in Latin-digit mono, like the app, so numbers look like Buddget before the logo appears.

What this avoids

Coins, rising arrows, stock people, neon gradients, piggy banks, calculators, and fear red. That's what makes it read as Buddget and not as generic fintech.

4 · Buddgy's role and rulesApproved in principleDetails pending approval

Recognisable, not in charge

Personality

  • Curious, not clever. He asks.
  • Calm. Never in a hurry, never alarmed.
  • Plainspoken. Short sentences, no jargon.
  • Quietly dry. A light joke now and then, never at anyone's expense.
  • Never judging. He doesn't have opinions about spending.

The credibility split

Buddgy asks. Buddget states. Questions, warmth and reactions come from Buddgy. Facts, claims and numbers come from the Buddget wordmark voice, or from a real screen. That keeps a finance product credible while the character adds personality.

Short bubbles of type, no cartoon voice. The helmet joke is a test of dry humour: say if it feels too cute.

Rules

  • Early on, in about one post in three; more only as the audience grows familiar.
  • Allowed moods: curious (asks), thinking (counting), done (clear), wink (dry humour), listening (Q&A), talking (explaining), sleepy (night sign-off).
  • Never alert, worried or error faces in awareness content.
  • Never gives financial advice, tips or predictions. Never mentions a number as a fact; numbers come from a real screen.
  • Existing artwork only. Movement comes from the existing mood animations.
  • Never the whole post. Never in every frame.

Recurring formats

  • Buddgy asks · a weekly question. From Act 1.
  • Buddgy listens · Stories Q&A. From Act 2.
  • Money notes · 30-second education; Buddgy only reacts and closes. From Act 2.
  • Buddgy explains · points at a real screen. Act 3, after the capability is verified.

How recognition is built

A repeated mini-arc of moods: curious (he asks) then thinking (counting) then done (it's clear). The audience learns it. Same sign-off spot, same teal face, same single question.

6 · Native Arabic reviewersApproved

Nothing in Arabic is published without one

Who

  • Egypt: a native Egyptian Arabic reviewer. Required first; blocks all Egypt content.
  • Saudi Arabia: a native Saudi reviewer.
  • UAE: a native Emirati or Gulf Arabic reviewer.

If three aren't affordable at once, Egyptian review comes first. Saudi and UAE content is not published until its own native review is done.

What they check

  • Natural phrasing and dialect
  • Cultural relevance
  • Humour
  • Financial terminology
  • Does it sound native, not translated
  • Anything religiously or socially sensitive, especially around Ramadan and Eid

Workflow (applies to every batch)

1 · Draft
AI-assisted draft in the market's dialect, checked against the claims register.
2 · Review
The native reviewer edits with tracked comments, against the checklist above.
3 · Revise
Changes applied; disagreements come to you.
4 · Approve
You give final approval, then it's produced and published.

For a batch of five posts, I'd ask for review within about two days, so it doesn't stall production (a working target, not a quote). Reviewer rates are unknown; get quotes before committing. A shared glossary of financial terms per dialect (balance, instalment, subscription, salary, "what's left") grows with each batch. Voice-over artists are a separate casting from reviewers.
