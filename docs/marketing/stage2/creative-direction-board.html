1 · The campaign idea
What's actually left?
فاضل فعلًا كام؟

Egyptian Arabic. Saudi and Gulf versions are in section 5.

The insight owner belief

Everyone checks the bank balance. The balance is the number before the instalments, the subscriptions, the rent and the goals. The question people really ask is not "how much is in the bank" but "how much is actually left".

To be validated in the planned interviews, not stated as a statistic.

The three acts

  • The question (problem awareness): dramatise the gap between the balance and what is left. No product.
  • One picture (brand): the answer is one picture of the whole month.
  • Show it (reveal onwards): real screens from a verified build.
A challenge to our own idea. "What's left" is close to the safe-to-spend claim that Masrofi, Masareef and Masareefy already make. We don't sell the calculation. We own the question, the tone and the broader picture, and we make no comparative claim. Before the reveal, the "left" figure in the tester build has to be verified.

Five signature devices

1 · Mist paper

A soft off-white ground and ink type, like a ledger page, not a glossy fintech screen.

2 · Ledger leaders

Dotted lines linking a label to its amount, as on a receipt. It echoes the coin b and carries the "adding it up" idea.

3 · The marker

One key word gets a soft teal highlight. One idea per post.

4 · One big question

Posts lead with a plain question or a plain sentence, not a statistic or a promise.

5 · Mono numbers

Amounts in mono Latin digits, matching the app, so the numbers look like Buddget before the logo appears.

What we avoid

Coins and rising arrows, stock people, neon gradients, calculator and piggy-bank clichés, and fear red. Alert red stays out of awareness content.

6 · Buddgy in the campaign

Recognisable, not in charge

Role

The one who asks the gentle question and signs off. He never lectures and never gives advice. The brand is Buddget, and Buddgy is its recurring signature.

Recognition device

The visor face in teal, the single question, the same sign-off spot in every post.

Rules

  • Early on, in about one post in three, rising as familiarity grows.
  • Moods: curious (asks), thinking (counting), done (clear), wink (light humour), listening (Stories), sleepy (night sign-off).
  • No alert, worried or error faces in awareness content, so he never signals fear.
  • He never mocks anyone's spending.
  • Existing artwork only. Motion comes from the existing mood animations, built by a motion editor.

Recurring formats: Buddgy asks (weekly question), Money notes (short education, later), sign-off (every video).

7 · How the name arrives

Buddget from the first post, explained slowly

Your decision stands: the name is used from the start. The sequence below is by campaign week, not tied to a launch date. Each step is gated by readiness, not by the calendar.

Weeks 1 to 2
Signature
The wordmark appears as the sign-off with the question. No explanation of what Buddget is. Goal: the name and the look become familiar.
Weeks 3 to 4
Voice
Buddget speaks as a calm narrator in short money notes. First Buddgy cameo at the end of a post.
Weeks 5 to 6
Meet Buddgy
A short introduction Reel and a question-box Story. Buddgy becomes a character, in his usual moods.
Weeks 7 to 8
We're building it
Behind the scenes and an honest call to action: the beta list on the website (it exists). The wording says "we're building Buddget", not that it's available.
Reveal
Gated
Real screens, one moment per video, only after the core workflows and the budget builder pass the launch gates in a build you've verified. No date, no price, no bank names.
