1 · Executive campaign overview
What's actually left?
فاضل كام فعلًا؟

The campaign in one paragraph

Buddget helps people understand, organise and take control of their money. The campaign asks the question people ask quietly every month, "what's actually left?", then widens it into the whole picture, then answers it on real screens. It starts with the name, a look and a voice, not with a feature or a download ask, and it keeps asking after launch.

What we are building toward

A recognisable, trusted, approachable personal-finance brand in Egypt first, then Saudi Arabia and the UAE. Recognition comes first, trust second, product third, conversion last.

DecisionLocked choice
MarketsEgypt primary; Saudi Arabia and UAE secondary and thin until Egypt works
PlatformsInstagram (brand home) and TikTok (discovery)
ProductionLean and AI-assisted; no person on camera; native Arabic review before publishing
BudgetNo fixed budget; organic first; paid only as small tests behind proven content
Launch dateNot set. Product readiness gates decide it. Never tied to Ramadan.
The honest state of readiness. No Buddget capability is verified as reliable enough to promise. Egypt bank-text capture is partial, Saudi and UAE capture are unverified, the tester build is unknown, and the app is left-to-right. The campaign is designed so its first stages need none of that. See section 14.
2 · Approved positioning and brand voice

Positioning (approved)

Buddget is a personal finance companion that helps people understand, organize, and take control of their money.

Core message: managing money doesn't have to be complicated. Buddget helps people get a clearer picture of their financial lives and make more informed decisions. No comparative claim against any competitor; SMS capture, safe-to-spend and Arabic are treated as entry tickets, not advantages.

Personality

  • Smart, never arrogant
  • Friendly, not childish
  • Confident, not aggressive
  • Reassuring, not boring
  • Modern and creative, not trend-obsessed
  • Practical and realistic about everyday money

The no-shame rule (non-negotiable)

  • Never make anyone feel stupid, irresponsible, poor or guilty.
  • No fear, no fearmongering about debt or spending.
  • No fake statistics, testimonials or promises about financial outcomes.
  • No get-rich framing, no financial advice.

The "we" voice (a craft decision from the Batch 1 critique)

Buddget speaks as we, not you: «بنفتح رصيد البنك», «بنحسب في دماغنا», «اللي معانا». It's shared, it keeps the no-shame rule honest, and it avoids assuming the viewer's gender, which Arabic otherwise forces.

6 · Buddgy character guidelines

Personality and role

  • Curious, calm, plainspoken, dryly warm, never judging.
  • Buddgy asks; Buddget states. Questions and reactions come from Buddgy. Facts, claims and numbers come from the Buddget voice or a real screen.
  • Test: with Buddgy removed, is it still a credible finance brand? If not, rewrite.
  • Strengthens recognition; never the main attraction.

Gender wording (final)

  • Use masculine grammatical wording where Arabic grammar requires it, consistently (for example «Buddgy بيسأل»).
  • Prefer the name. Avoid pronouns and unnecessary gender references. In English, use "Buddgy".
  • Gender is never a marketing message. No backstory, no "he/she" jokes.
  • Established personality and artwork unchanged. No redesign.

When Buddgy appears

  • Act 1: about one post in three, small, as a closing beat. In Batch 1 he appears once (post 3).
  • Act 2: up to one post in two while the character is introduced, then settles back.
  • Act 3: as a pointer in demos; never covering the screen.
  • Never in every frame, never the whole post.

How Buddgy communicates

  • Short type bubbles, 12 words or fewer, mostly questions.
  • No cartoon voice, no sound effects, no emoji.
  • Moods: curious, thinking, done, wink, listening, talking, sleepy.
  • Never alert, worried or error faces in awareness content.
  • Existing artwork and mood animations only.

Humour allowed

  • Dry observation about a situation: "Five places, one salary. Even I'd need a minute." خمس أماكن لمرتب واحد. حتى أنا هحتاج دقيقة.
  • Self-inclusive understatement; playful numbers.

Humour not allowed

  • Anything about Buddgy's body or appearance (the helmet joke is retired).
  • Being poor, broke or bad with money; sarcasm at the viewer; trend memes; religion, family pressure or giving.

How they work together

  • One sign-off in the same place on every post.
  • The same "we" voice and the same question at the end of every video.
  • Day 1 asks, Day 2 shows where the money goes, Day 3 shows why the balance misleads, Day 5 listens, Day 6 shows what hides. The audience ends the week with a recognisable brand and a clear problem, and nothing about a product.
  • The three problems left for Batch 2: saving without knowing what is free, several instalments, and not knowing what is safe to spend.

What the self-critique cut

  • The cashier listicle ("3 kinds of maths"): a generic format, and it overlapped post 3.
  • Repeated insight: "the balance isn't what's left" is now said once (post 3).
  • Second-person address: replaced by "we", for tone and gender neutrality.
  • Buddgy in post 5: cut; he adds nothing there.
  • "We're building Buddget" in the carousel: cut; it belongs to stage 4.
